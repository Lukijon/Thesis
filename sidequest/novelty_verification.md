# Novelty verification: does anyone in Brazil already test board racial diversity against a firm outcome?

A second, independent research pass, specifically instructed to try to **disprove** the novelty claim in `README.md` rather than confirm it — same discipline this whole project applies to its own statistical findings, applied here to a literature claim instead. ~30 additional queries across Portuguese and English sources, CVM resolution numbers, five named Brazilian journals, BDTD (Brazil's thesis database), SSRN, and adjacent framings.

## Verdict: claim holds up. Confidence ~85–90%, not absolute.

No study — in Brazil or about Brazil — was found that regresses CVM-mandated board/executive racial diversity data against any firm financial outcome (performance, risk, cost of capital, valuation). The closest near-miss actually **reinforces** the gap rather than closing it: IBGC (a governance institute, not an academic source) published a 2025 report using the *actual* regulatory data — 390 companies, FY2024 — but it stops at description (82.6% white, 3.4% pardo, 1.1% amarelo, 0.5% preto in leadership roles). No outcome variable, no regression. Two academic papers that use the same CVM Formulário de Referência instrument for other diversity questions (Nisiyama & Nakamura 2018 on leverage; Ventura, Matos & Nasu 2024, a 2012–2021 longitudinal diversity study) **explicitly do not include race** as a variable — the second because its sample window predates the disclosure mandate entirely.

## Real residual uncertainty, stated plainly

- **One thesis remains genuinely unverified**: Neves (FECAP, MSc), defended ~2 weeks before this search, titled around "CEO discursive positioning" on diversity. Every access attempt failed. Title suggests rhetoric/discourse analysis, not a financial-outcome regression — but that's an inference from a title, not a confirmation.
- **BDTD (Brazil's thesis database) blocked every direct-content fetch** with a bot wall — searches had to work from snippets/titles only, not full abstracts. A very recent, unusually-titled thesis could be sitting there unseen.
- Conference proceedings (ANPCONT, EnANPAD, AdCont 2024–2026) were searched via general web queries, not crawled paper-by-paper.
- The regulatory data itself is young (first real disclosures landed in 2023 filings, a fuller second wave in 2025 filings for FY2024) — mechanically limits how much time anyone has had to publish on it, which cuts *in favor of* the claim being true almost by construction.

## What actually makes this gap credible, not just "unsearched"

The most useful evidence is the international contrast, not the absence of a Brazilian hit. Multiple countries with comparable or *weaker* disclosure regimes already have this literature:

- **South Africa**: two independent 2024–2025 studies (JSE data) regressing board racial diversity against shareholder value/Tobin's Q and REIT-specific FFO/EPS.
- **Malaysia** (2020, ethnic diversity → earnings quality), **Indonesia** (2024, ethnicity of president commissioner moderates the diversity-performance link), **USA** (2024 natural experiment via California's AB 979 mandate; 2025 CEO-pay-gap study), **Australia** (2024, CSR outcomes), and a **12-country European study** (2026, ethnic-minority CEOs and firm risk) all exist.

Brazil has arguably richer data than most of these (a single mandatory, standardized national disclosure, not fragmented voluntary reporting) and no equivalent study yet. That combination — good data, comparable countries already publishing, nothing found for Brazil — is what makes this a credible gap rather than just an artifact of imperfect search.
